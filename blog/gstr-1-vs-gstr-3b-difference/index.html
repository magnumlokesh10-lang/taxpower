GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B: what each return reports and how they should match

By Siddharth Enterprises · Published 23 October 2026 · Last updated 23 October 2026 · 4 min read

GSTR-1 lists your outward supplies invoice by invoice. GSTR-3B is the summary return in which you declare liability, claim input tax credit (ITC) and pay tax. The sales figures in the two returns should agree.

The short difference

GSTR-1GSTR-3B
What it reportsOutward supplies (sales), invoice-wise and by typeSummary of outward supplies, ITC claimed and tax payable
PurposeGives your buyers their GSTR-2A/2B data and the department your sales detailDeclares liability and is the return through which tax is paid
Monthly due date11th of the next month20th of the next month
Quarterly (QRMP) due date13th of the month after the quarter22nd or 24th of the month after the quarter, by state category
Who it affectsYour customers, because their ITC depends on itYou, because tax is paid with it

Why your customers care about GSTR-1

The invoices you report in GSTR-1 flow into your buyers' auto-drafted GSTR-2B. If an invoice is missed or reported with the wrong GSTIN or value, your buyer may not see the credit they expect. That leads to calls from customers asking you to correct the return.

Why the two returns must agree

GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B are compared by the system. In recent years GSTN has moved towards auto-populating GSTR-3B from GSTR-1, so an error in GSTR-1 can carry into GSTR-3B. A mismatch between sales in the two returns can lead to a notice, so it is worth checking before you file.

A quick pre-filing check

  • Compare total taxable value and tax in GSTR-1 with the outward supply table in GSTR-3B
  • Check that credit notes and amendments are included in the right period
  • Look at invoices reported in the wrong month or under the wrong GSTIN
  • Confirm that e-invoices match what you report in GSTR-1
  • Keep a note of the reason for any difference you decide to carry forward
